# Mind.Media — Concept, Revenue Model & Competitive Benchmark

## 1. The winning concept

**Mind.Media = a mental-wellbeing media brand + a referral engine.**
Content, video and free tools pull in traffic; a matching service turns that traffic into high-value leads.

**Why this and not the alternatives**

| Option | Traffic potential | RPM / value per visitor | Lead-gen value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General "mind/brain" news blog | Medium | Low–mid | Weak | Commodity, no moat |
| Meditation app clone | Low (app-dependent) | Low | Weak | Needs capital, native apps |
| Psychology/mind video channel only | High | Mid (YouTube split) | Weak | No owned audience |
| **Wellbeing media + therapist/coach/B2B matching** | **High** | **High (health ad categories pay well)** | **Very high** | **Chosen** |

**What the data says.** In Similarweb's mental-health category ranking (Aug 2026), the top sites split into two groups:
- **Content + directory:** psychologytoday.com (~2:42 average visit, 3.18 pages/visit), verywellmind.com, calm.com.
- **Matching/booking platforms:** headway.co, growtherapy.com, helloalma.com, rula.com, talkiatry.com, betterhelp.com. These show long sessions (3.5–7 minutes) and 4–7 pages per visit, which is what you see when people are close to buying.

Psychology Today's model is the template: free editorial content brings in traffic, and paid therapist listings earn the money. Mind.Media copies that model and adds tools and video for engagement.

## 2. Revenue stack (priority order)

1. **Lead generation (main engine)**
   - B2C: therapy and coaching match requests. Sell them to practitioners per lead, or route them to affiliate programs such as online-therapy platforms.
   - B2B: workplace wellbeing proposal requests, which are worth far more per lead.
   - Supply side: practitioner listing plans (Basic free, then Pro and Partner subscriptions).
2. **Google AdSense.** Health and wellbeing content usually earns above-average RPMs. There are 5 ad slots per template, and ads load only after cookie consent.
3. **YouTube.** An embedded video hub, plus a CTA to your own channel for AdSense on YouTube and sponsor reads.
4. **Sponsorships.** Newsletter, tool "presented by" placements, contest prize funding, category exclusivity.
5. **Reader support.** Memberships and donations directed to operations, promotion, talent or contests.
6. **Affiliates.** Books, sleep products, meditation apps, courses (add later).

## 4. Trademark note

Several unrelated businesses use "Mind Media," including a Netherlands biofeedback-equipment maker, US marketing and creative agencies, and a French media page. To stay clear of them, Mind.Media:
- always writes the brand with the dot, as the domain name, and describes it as "media for the mind";
- does not offer biofeedback hardware or agency services;
- carries a non-affiliation and takedown disclosure (`legal.html`) plus a one-line footer disclaimer on every page.

Before scaling paid marketing, run a formal clearance search (USPTO, CIPO, EUIPO, WIPO Global Brand Database). This is not legal advice.
